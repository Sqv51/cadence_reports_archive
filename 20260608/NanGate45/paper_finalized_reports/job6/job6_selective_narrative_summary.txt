Job 6 Selective Steering Summary Notes
=====================================

The Job 6 campaign evaluated a selective mule-steering policy across the
original 6 application kernels and the 14 latency-hiding kernels used for the
extended Table 14 study. Power was extracted by replaying RTL VCD activity onto
the finalized core_hybrid_100 post-route database.

Scope and completeness:
- 20 benchmarks were evaluated.
- 40 simulation metrics files were collected: baseline and selective for every
  benchmark.
- 40 post-route power reports were collected: baseline and selective for every
  benchmark.

Key verification outcomes:
- fir_unrolled is no longer 100% mule under the selective policy.
- matmul_tiled is no longer naively over-patched; the selective policy chose
  zero replacements and preserved baseline timing behavior.
- The policy fixed the original dense-loop failure mode, but the unrolled_dot16,
  unrolled_dot32, and unrolled_dot64 kernels still behave like dynamically
  all-mule kernels because each exposes a single hot static multiply site.

Top-level observations:
- The lowest cycle penalty is 0.000%, achieved by fir_direct, matmul_tiled,
  outer_product, and sliding_correlation.
- Among kernels that were genuinely steered, fft_butterfly had the lowest cycle
  penalty at +1.803%.
- The largest total core power reduction was observed on unrolled_dot16 at
  -1.400%.
- Several synthetic latency-hiding kernels reduced core power modestly, but the
  cost in cycles remains material for the estrin and unrolled-dot families.

Recommended interpretation for paper notes:
- The selective policy is materially safer than the naive 100% replacement flow.
- It prevents the worst structural hazard cases on the original benchmark set.
- Further refinement is still needed for singleton hot-loop multiply kernels,
  where static patch counts understate the dynamic all-mule behavior.
